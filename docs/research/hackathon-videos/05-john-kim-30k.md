# Video 5 — "I Made $30,000 From Winning Hackathons, Here's How!"

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mEFi5DiA0tM (John Kim, 8:14)
- **Speaker:** John Kim, software engineer at Meta; over $30K in hackathon prizes, including at TechCrunch Disrupt
- **Notes taken:** 31 October 2026, from YouTube's auto-generated English transcript

## Core claim

Hackathons are sponsored marketing events. Companies pay to advertise their technology to developers. **Winning means showcasing the sponsor's technology prominently and compellingly**, not writing the most elegant code.

## Sponsor strategy

- Don't go in "guns blazing" on a random cool idea without talking to anyone.
- The people at sponsor booths are often the judges for sponsor prizes.
- **TechCrunch Disrupt story:** Esri's JS map SDK had an animation feature their mobile SDK lacked. He asked "if we build that and show it in a compelling app, would you pick us?" They built it overnight and won $5,000.
- Treat it as market analysis: know what your "customer" (the sponsor) wants before writing code.

## Roles for non-coders

- **Product manager:** talks to sponsors, combines their APIs coherently, pitches ideas to them for a reaction, and writes the product spec.
- **Presenter:** a strong 3–5 minute presentation is about **half the project**. A winning presentation makes the sponsor look amazing.

## Execution

- **Work backwards from the presentation.** Decide the key points and features to showcase first, and make sure they highlight the sponsor's tech.
- Mock where it makes sense ("it's a hack, not production software").
- But a large part must really work: **sponsors check the repo** to confirm you built something real, not just slides.
