# Video 1 — "How I Won 11 Hackathons | Complete Guide"

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YwANe-1W8iU (Scaler School of Technology, 13:53)
- **Speaker:** Zahur, student; won 11 of 15 hackathons; first one placed 53rd of 100
- **Notes taken:** 31 October 2026, from YouTube's auto-generated English transcript

## Core claim

Hackathons are a competition of product decisions, with clear assumptions about the user and the metric, that can be explained and demoed fast. They are not a coding-volume contest. His losing first project was a feature-heavy skincare app with a custom ML model.

## Framework

0. **Choose the hackathon.** Check tech stack, problem statements, theme and judges against your direction.
1. **Research the problem, not the idea.** Never ask an AI "what should I build?". Use it to research the industry's problem: size, frequency, money/time lost, existing solutions.
2. **Specific user persona.** "Everyone" is the worst answer. Name the exact segment, then remove friction in *their* journey (farmers → a phone number with an AI agent, not an app).
3. **Unique solution, few features.** Three features that solve the problem beat 15. Optimise the USP. For every feature ask: who uses this, and is it core or nice-to-have? Build integrations (import/export) that fit the user's existing tools.
4. **Sell it in the pitch.** One teammate keeps asking "how will we show this in the demo?" while building.

## Pitch deck order

1. Problem + target user, with an **audience question** as an icebreaker
2. **Evidence:** surveys, reports, news articles
3. **Cost of the problem:** money or time lost
4. **Solution:** home screen + a description a non-technical person understands (no jargon)
5. **Live demo** in the exact order the ideal user would use it; core feature first, explain *why* it is built that way
6. **Before/after:** "13 hours → 3 hours"
7. Architecture / tech / team split (optional)
8. Team

## Q&A

- Listen fully, stay calm, do not argue.
- Know your USP, features and competitors exactly.
- **Never lie about unbuilt features.** Say "time-bound; here is what we built, here is what is next."
