# Video 2 — "He Won 14 Hackathons. Here's Everything He Knows."

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P9T_lNxksKg (Not Your College, 22:34)
- **Speaker:** Vaibhav (interviewed); 14 wins, first win at IIT Delhi
- **Notes taken:** 31 October 2026, from YouTube's auto-generated English transcript

## Core claim

Winning is a repeatable process, not luck. A weaker tech with a clear story often beats a stronger tech. "An ugly working prototype beats a beautiful non-working prototype."

## Choosing events and teams

- Read the problem statements before saying yes. Prefer offline events and track-wise judging.
- Team = tech + non-tech. A "glue" person (PPT, marketing, pitching) is as important as frontend/backend. Four frontend friends is useless.
- Teams fail from ego clashes and unclear leadership, even with strong individuals.

## Ideation

- Balance originality against **what you can execute in the time**. A great idea you cannot build is useless.
- **Lock the idea in the first 1–2 hours.** Indecision kills time (his team was still deciding at 11 for a 12 o'clock deadline).
- Must be explainable **in one sentence**. Wow factor helps, but clarity matters more.
- Beware the bubble: an idea feels good to you but may not need to exist.

## Execution

- MVP = **one flow** that explains how the idea works. Cover must-haves; nice-to-haves become "future scope".
- Scope the architecture to a small audience (a video platform for one group, not a full streaming platform).
- **Build the riskiest/hardest part first**; UI polish later.
- **Cut anything that is not in the demo.**
- **Integrate continuously** — wire each feature into the UI as soon as it is done. Integrating at the end is a top failure mode.
- Stuck debugging a feature under time pressure? Cut the feature.
- Avoid unfamiliar tech chosen only to look cool.

## Final hours and pitch

- Freeze features; lock and rehearse the pitch.
- Problem → why it matters → solution → live demo → tech stack/business plan. **Never open with the tech stack.**
- Most confident person pitches (not necessarily the lead). One speaker; multiple speakers confuse judges.
- Have a fallback plan. Identify the **one flow that must work live**; if it might break, record it / screenshot it (if organisers allow). Don't fake data or hard-code results. Don't show features you can't guarantee live.

## AI usage

- Use AI for boilerplate and debugging. It is "a dumb decision" not to.
- Danger: building something with AI you can't explain. Judges ask niche "how did you implement this?" questions.

## Q&A

- Anticipate: why this tech stack, who is the target audience.
- Don't know? Pivot briefly to what you do know. Team must know the architecture cold. **Never bluff.**

## Loss story

A blockchain agriculture supply chain for farmers lost because farmers often lack phones that can run it. Technically strong, not usable in real life.

## Unique to this video

- Team composition and the "glue" role
- Lock idea in 1–2 hours
- Riskiest part first; integrate continuously; cut a feature instead of endless debugging
- Single speaker; fallback plan; record the one critical flow
- AI-usage rule: you must be able to explain what AI built
