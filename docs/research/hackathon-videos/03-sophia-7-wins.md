# Video 3 — "How to win a hackathon (tips from a 7x winner & organizer!)"

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DizrlqWIEWs (Sophia Sharif, 8:13)
- **Speaker:** Sophia Sharif; 7-hackathon win streak; former tech director of LA Hacks (set up its judging and picked winners)
- **Notes taken:** 31 October 2026, from YouTube's auto-generated English transcript

## Core claim

Judges know your project **only** through the 2–5 minute demo. They don't see your repo or your 24 hours. Everything you want credited must be communicated in the demo.

## How judging works (organiser view)

- General prizes vs sponsor prizes; general prizes are judged only on the demo.
- LA Hacks / Stanford / Berkeley / MIT use **Gavel**: pairwise comparison against the previous project a judge saw, Elo-style. Mostly no rubric — "vibes".
- Judges are impressed by three things: **technical complexity, impact, completeness.**
- When picking finalists she had 30–60 minutes for all top submissions. **People mostly watch the demo video and often don't read the description.**

## Ideation

- For each candidate idea, write the **demo path first**, then judge the idea as if you were a judge watching that demo. Not "how cool or ambitious does it feel".

## The common demo mistake

Opening with a statistic, describing the product abstractly, then rushing 2–3 disconnected features at the end. Judges leave with a theoretical understanding and aren't convinced it solves the problem.

## The demo structure she uses now

- A **movie**: problem → moment of tension → solution.
- The solution takes **~90% of the time** (4.5 of 5 minutes).
- Show features **chronologically, as one story** ("a gunshot just went off; watch what happens"), not as isolated capabilities.

## Execution

- From the demo path, split features into essential (demo breaks without it) and nice-to-have.
- Always underestimate how much you will get done.
- Split work into independent chunks with explicit **API contracts** between components.
- Use **libraries** — judges can't tell library code from yours, so you get more done.
- Add **visuals** (maps, images, video, hardware, equations); they signal complexity without words.
- Don't be a perfectionist; **simplify** when you hit problems (precise triangulation → nearest microphone).

## Final 2–3 hours

Start the **demo video** rather than finishing one more small feature.

## Unique to this video

- Organiser's view of judging (Gavel / pairwise, no rubric, little time per project)
- Judges mostly skip descriptions → the demo video is the product
- Pick ideas by writing the demo path first
- Demo as a chronological story, ~90% on the solution
- API contracts, libraries, visuals
