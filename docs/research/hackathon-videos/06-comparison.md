## Where they agree

All five land on the same core: **a narrow, real problem, a few working features, and a demo-led story decide the result.** Raw technical depth matters only as far as the demo makes it visible.

## Where they differ

- **Repo vs demo:** Sophia (general prizes, big US events) says judges never see the repo. John Kim (sponsor prizes) says sponsors do check it. Both hold for their prize type. For a Devpost entry with a required repository, assume both: the video sells it and the repo must hold up.
- **Opening:** Zahur opens with an audience question plus evidence and cost slides. Sophia warns that a statistic-led, startup-style opening leaves judges with a theory instead of a story. Reconcile by keeping the problem short and concrete, then spending most of the time on the solution.
- **Mocking:** John Kim accepts mocks ("a hack, not production"). Vaibhav warns against faking data or hard-coding. Common ground: mock the non-core parts, keep the core flow real, and never present a mock as real.
- **Lens:** Zahur and Vaibhav think like product people, Sophia like a judge, John Kim like a sponsor. Jake Kang shows what goes wrong in practice.
