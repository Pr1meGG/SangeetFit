## 5. Pitfalls: 10 overused Indian-wedding logo clichés to avoid

These are design-risk judgments, not a statistically measured “ten most common” ranking. Cultural motifs themselves are not the problem; unmodified stock treatments and unsupported symbolism are.

1. **Generic radial mandala.** A repeated petal count is not a brand idea. It can read as yoga, spirituality, beauty or wedding stationery. For Ghera, preserve a decisive cloth seam and direction of motion.
2. **Clip-art peacock or feather.** Strong regional familiarity, weak distinction. A many-eyed tail becomes noise at small size and crowds the name.
3. **Stock lotus.** Carries associations beyond celebration and overlaps an established jewellery vocabulary. A new petal arrangement alone will not explain SangeetFit.
4. **Gold-gradient swirls.** The finish performs all the work. A symbol must remain recognisable as black ink before it earns a foil treatment.
5. **Om/Ganesha/deity used as decoration.** Introduces specific religious meaning into a broad outfit app without a brand reason. Do not turn sacred signs into arbitrary flourishes or invent a blessing story.
6. **Bride-and-groom silhouette, doli or baraat scene.** Communicates wedding services while making a guest-oriented, multi-outfit product seem bridal-only. Scene detail also fails at favicon scale.
7. **Crown plus laurel plus shield plus “royal” initials.** Borrowed status without provenance; familiar hotel, boutique or jewellery template. Learn proportion from heraldry without fabricating a family crest.
8. **Hanger, mannequin or measuring tape hidden in an S.** Literal clothing utility, little celebration or couture character; can read as alterations or fitness.
9. **Script initials encircled by a wreath.** Wedding-invitation grammar. The locked script Fit already supplies calligraphy; the symbol should add a different, meaningful idea.
10. **Every reference combined.** Bell + flower + leaf + dancer + AI sparkle is an illustration checklist. Choose one dominant construction and make the remaining meaning emerge from its relationships.

A useful counterexample: Versace shows that luxury need not mean minimalist. The brief's 16 px requirement, rather than a rule against ornament, is what requires reduction here. [Versace's Medusa house code](https://www.versace.com/us/en/stories/advertising-campaigns/versace-house-code-medusa-95.html).
